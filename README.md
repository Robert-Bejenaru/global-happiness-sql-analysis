# Exploring Global Happiness with SQL

## Objective

Explore how national happiness scores relate to socioeconomic and social indicators, including GDP per capita, social support, healthy life expectancy, freedom, generosity and perceptions of corruption.

## Dataset

The project analyses **156 countries** and reports these overall averages:

- Global happiness score: **5.41**
- GDP per capita: **0.91**
- Social support: **1.21**
- Healthy life expectancy: **0.73**
- Freedom to make life choices: **0.39**
- Generosity: **0.19**
- Perceptions of corruption: **0.11**

## Tools

- SQL
- Data aggregation, ranking and comparison

## Analysis Completed

- Compared all countries by happiness score.
- Identified the top 10 and bottom 10 countries.
- Calculated global averages across the main indicators.
- Compared GDP per capita with global happiness.
- Ranked countries by social support.
- Ranked countries by healthy life expectancy.
- Compared corruption perception with happiness.
- Compared generosity for above- and below-average happiness groups.
- Identified countries with the largest difference between GDP rank and happiness rank.

## Key Findings

- Higher happiness scores in the analysed corruption comparison were concentrated around corruption-perception values of roughly **0.28–0.40**.
- The analysis identified a notable outlier with a corruption-perception value of **0.41** and a happiness score of **3.33**.
- Average generosity differed by only **0.01** between the above-average and below-average happiness groups, suggesting generosity was not a strong discriminator in this analysis.
- Hong Kong had a GDP rank of **9** and a happiness rank of **76**, a difference of **67 places**.
- Guatemala, El Salvador, Nicaragua and Uzbekistan ranked substantially higher in happiness than in GDP, illustrating that economic position alone did not explain the happiness rankings in this dataset.

## Recommendations

- Consider social support, health and governance alongside GDP when evaluating national well-being.
- Investigate how corruption perception relates to quality-of-life outcomes.
- Avoid interpreting these relationships as causal without additional longitudinal or contextual data.
